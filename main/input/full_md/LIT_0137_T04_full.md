## A B S T R A C T

The launch of ChatGPT by OpenAI reflected a significant turning point in the search and man agement of diversified information in all fields of knowledge. The concurrent use and ethical awareness of ChatGPT among pharmacy students are examined in detail in this study. During the academic year 2023–2024, a cross-sectional survey was conducted with 413 pharmacy students from different universities in Saudi Arabia. An online questionnaire evaluated their perception and impressions, as well as literacy levels about ChatGPT, together with its proper applications in this professional field of study. Based on the awareness levels of ChatGPT, participants were split into two groups for comparison. It was revealed that a sizable proportion of students (62.55%) were unaware of the concepts and their use. However, students with prior experience using ChatGPT exhibited higher levels of awareness. Nonetheless, on pharmacy-related issues, only 57.7% of participants thought that ChatGPT’s responses and information were either somewhat or to some extent appropriate. However, a number of participants (52.3%) had doubts and worries regarding the using ChatGPT. The majority of the participants (78.0%) thought that ChatGPT would have a beneficial effect on pharmacy education regardless of the pending concerns. Only 1.2% and 2.7% of total participants correctly identified the problematic and appropriate applications of ChatGPT, respectively, which indicated an insufficient literacy and ethical awareness about the use of ChatGPT. These results demonstrated a critical need for improved education and training for ChatGPT use, which also warranted more precise recommendations for ethical applications of ChatGPT. It is imperative to fill the educational gaps for a better profession-bound ethical use of ChatGPT. The current ChatGPT use status, including current levels of ethical considerations, guidelines for better ethical use, prospects, and recommendations for enhancing ChatGPT use, are outlined.

## Introduction

A Generative Pre-Trained Transformer (GPT) is a type of large language model that creates text, answers prompt, and converses dynamically.<sup>1,2</sup> ChatGPT is arguably the most used application of GPT, and its numerous academic and professional uses speak to that. The model’s capabilities in natural language understanding and contextually relevant responses have attracted the attention of learners and educators alike, especially in the health disciplines. Incorporating ChatGPT in healthcare education demands the reassessment of conventional evaluation approaches. Generating tailored clinical cases and enhancing topics’ comprehensibility, providing fresh possibilities for boosting problem-based learning, and critical thinking.<sup>2–5</sup> The benefits of adopting ChatGPT in academic and scientific writing have been highlighted in several studies. Such benefits involve strengthening language competence and offering nonstop access to information. Additionally, ChatGPT assists students in research, generating academic papers, and being well-prepared for exams and assignments without drawing the notice of software that detects plagiarism, which goes beyond accepted ethical norms.<sup>7</sup> A recent study demonstrated ChatGPT’s groundbreaking potential in healthcare education. This technology, powered by Artificial Intelligence (AI), potentially transforms the way curricula are tailored in real time. Educators may also utilize ChatGPT to bridge the divide between the rapidly expanding fields of medicine and educational frameworks arena. ChatGPT empowers healthcare students to participate in discussions by offering valuable feedback, stimulating enquiries, and insightful observations in virtual environments.<sup>8</sup> Thus, the AI-mediated ChatGPT-based debate encouraged pharmacy students to explore multiple points of view, tactics, and medical concepts, thereupon resulting in a more dynamic and participatory concurrent pharmacy education.

ChatGPT has additionally shown excellent performance in plotting a route for several healthcare assessments, including the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE), Saudi Medical Licensing Exam (SMLE), postgraduate ophthalmology examination, medical student parasitology exam, and the American Heart Association (AHA) life support exam.<sup>9–13</sup> ChatGPT has also been shown to be more precise in exploring and being trustworthy than the pharmacists in handling factual knowledge questions, implying that it may be helpful for pharmacists. However, it is crucial to consider that ChatGPT’s information may not always reflect the most current developments, particularly in rapidly evolving fields like healthcare. Nonetheless, ChatGPT has the potential to improve the learning journey in an array of educational applications, including problem-based learning (PBL), team-based learning (TBL), and case-based learning (CBL).<sup>15</sup> Therefore, using ChatGPT enhance analytical abilities and encourages collaborative team decision-making at its best.<sup>8</sup> However, it remains imperative to acknowledge the inherent limitations and ethical concerns associated with ChatGPT. These include the potential for biased content generation, transparency and data privacy challenges, susceptibility to manipulation, and the risk of producing inequitable outputs. Copyright and accountability issues often give rise to legal concerns as well.<sup>2,5,16–19</sup> A phenomenon called "hallucination" may transpire when ChatGPT produces erroneous scientific data that seems plausible to individuals lacking sufficient understanding of the topic. This is where the model generates responses based on patterns in training data without actual comprehension, and this can mislead users who lack sufficient background knowledge on the topic.<sup>20,21</sup> Sallam et. al. (2023) identified risk perceptions, usefulness, convenience of use, attitudes towards technology, and behavioral factors are significant considerations when implementing ChatGPT as a tool in healthcare education.<sup>22</sup> Therefore, to guarantee that ChatGPT is used appropriately, many educational institutions worldwide have created standards and guidelines for students to ensure that ChatGPT is utilized responsibly in learning environments. Higher education educators are prepared to incorporate generative artificial intelligence into their curricula to promote professional growth, collaborative learning, and creative teaching methods.<sup>23</sup> In this context, the ethical use of ChatGPT has assumed significant propensity, but currently, there are only a limited number of studies investigating the ethical perspectives of ChatGPT use among a large section of students in healthcare education, while most researchers are now focusing primarily on medical students, the concentration on specific fields of education, such as, allied health sciences, nursing and pharmacy are lacking. Some studies have highlighted the ethical implications of ChatGPT use in medical education, but there is a notable lack of comprehensive, in-depth, specific, and fully addressing the pharmacy students’ perspectives on ChatGPT use. Existing literature emphasizes the perceptions of ChatGPT’s utility and its effectiveness in educational settings, but it does not investigate in detail the ethical considerations and knowledge gaps that students may have regarding ChatGPT applications in their respective fields of pharmacy education.

This study investigated how pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia perceive the use of ChatGPT through assessments of their beliefs and attitudes toward ChatGPT applications. It addresses the existing literature gaps, ethical expectations, and societal concerns. It also provides valuable insights for educators, policymakers, and institutions that are seeking to implement innovative ChatGPT tools as effectively as needed locally, and perhaps globally. Future outlines and conscientious, professional and rightful, correct use of ChatGPT are also discussed.

## Methodology

## Research questions and hypothesis

This study explores Saudi Arabian pharmacy students’ knowledge, attitude, and ethical awareness of using ChatGPT. It aims to find out how well the students are aware of the fundamental concepts of AI such as natural language processing and machine learning, and whether they can describe the roles and applications of ChatGPT. Moreover, the study investigates how students perceive the ethical aspects of using Al tools both in academic and professional environments. The research maintains that students who can define ChatGPT tend to hold favorable beliefs regarding its usefulness and express high levels of literacy. Furthermore, it hypothesizes that the degree of academia specifically the difference between interns and students in their lower years, can significantly affect students’ ethics and knowledge. These hypotheses attempt to delineate areas of Al literacy gaps and serve as the foundation for policy development and educational interventions in pharmacy education.

## Study design, settings, and population

A cross-sectional study design was conducted to investigate the ethical concerns among Saudi Arabian pharmacy students using ChatGPT. This study design was chosen for its effectiveness in obtaining a snapshot of their knowledge and moral perspectives. The Regional Research Ethics Committee in Qassim Province is registered by the National Committee of Bio and Medical Ethics (NCBE), Registration No. H-04-Q-001 on April 1, 2024, and authorized the study ethically. Before administering the survey, participants were oriented to the purpose of the research and given assurances about the privacy of their names. They were made aware that participation in the study was entirely optional. The completion of the questionnaire implied consent. The study targeted pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia during the 2023-2024 academic year, specifically from April 1 to April 20, 2024.

The research aimed to include a broad student population from various regions to analyze the targeted criteria thoroughly. Participants were eligible if they met the criteria: pharmacy students currently enrolled in academic years two through five, or pharmacy interns in Saudi Arabia during the study period, and having at least basic familiarity with ChatGPT. The study excluded non-pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia and pharmacy students in academic years outside of 2 to intern years. To ensure regional and academic diversity, a systematic selection, which formed a selection process to capture the regional and institutional diversity of pharmacy schools across Saudi Arabia’s all major regions (e.g., Central, Western, Eastern, Southern, Northern) were represented, thereby ensuring representation from schools with varying curricular structures. The approach aligned with the study’s aim of achieving a comprehensive representation of pharmacy students nationwide. The surveys were distributed to students within these schools in an equi-distribution pattern (nearly 20% in each region) through online survey platforms and were collaborated with faculty members at each institution to share the survey link within their networks. This method was chosen due to its efficiency in distribution widespread use among the student population.

## Sample size and techniques

In Saudi Arabia, over 3,000 pharmacy students graduate annually.<sup>24</sup> This study aimed to assess 300 pharmacy students, equating to approximately 10% of all pharmacy graduates. The study aimed to include students from all pharmacy schools across Saudi Arabia to ensure a comprehensive and representative sample. Pharmacy schools were systematically selected to provide regional diversity and include institutions with varying academic structures to reflect a broad perspective of pharmacy students’ knowledge and ethics. Given the diverse educational backgrounds of the target population, the authors divided the task of reaching the target group according to the regions of the pharmacy schools. The stratified random sampling method is essential for understanding the pharmacy student population comprehensively, considering inequalities in knowledge and moral issues across various strata. The study’s findings are more likely relevant to all pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia due to the assurance of varied regional and academic year coverage. This study’s ’academic level’ includes pharmacy interns as a separate category to reflect their advanced training stage, distinct from their undergraduate years. This categorization provides insights into the knowledge and ethical awareness of those transitioning from academic learning to professional practice.

## Data collection

Google Forms, a website program, was used to create the Likert-scale and multiple-choice responses to the online survey. To guarantee maximum clarity and applicability, an AI investigation specialist checked the questions and provided feedback, which allowed for the refinement and categorization of the questions. Then, to accommodate the linguistic diversity of the participants, the questionnaire was initially drafted in English and translated into Arabic by a bilingual expert to eliminate any ambiguities in wording. Another healthcare professional then verified the translation to ensure linguistic and contextual accuracy. The questionnaire was pretested with a pilot group of 10 pharmacy students, and their feedback was used to refine it further. The study questionnaire was distributed through widely utilized social media platforms to maximize participant reach and engagement.

The survey was divided into three parts, the first being the evaluation of pharmacy students’ perception of AI, and concentrating on their understanding of fundamental conceptions like raw language processing, machine learning, deep learning, and neural networks; the second, the evaluation of pharmacy students’ beliefs and impressions of ChatGPT; and third, the evaluation of literacy regarding the suitable usefulness of ChatGPT.<sup>4</sup> The questions were primarily developed based on the study’s objectives and followed the guidelines and questionnaire types, subjective domains, direction, depths, and concerns, which are referenced appropriately, and available in the AI literature.

## Data analyses

The alpha coefficient (Cronbach’s alpha) was utilized to estimate the psychometric scale’s reliability and the questionnaire items’ internal consistency. The results were presented and analyzed. The data were introduced and examined in the layout of explicative method.

At the outset, no prior background information on ChatGPT was provided to participants before the survey to ensure that participants’ responses reflected their natural level of awareness and perception. This approach was chosen to capture the baseline knowledge without inserting any external influences from the study investigators. Further, the participants were split into two clusters based on their answers to a question concerning their understanding of ChatGPT. Group 1 consisted of participants who self-reported that they could define ChatGPT. At the same time, Group 2 included participants who either self-reported being unable to clarify ChatGPT or indicated that they had never heard of it. Participants in Group 2 were included to assess baseline awareness and perceptions of AI tools, such as ChatGPT, even among those who were unfamiliar with its use. This approach provided insights into the knowledge gaps and challenges that may influence future educational interventions and policy development regarding ChatGPT. The respondents’ features were corresponded and interpreted in terms of their understanding of the AI, their belief in ChatGPT, and their literacy concerning the suitable usefulness of ChatGPT.

Section 1 consisted of four three-point Likert-scale inquiries. The scoring measures were as follows: -1 = "I have no idea, " 0 = "I have heard of it, but I cannot explain it," and 1 = "I know the term and can explain it". The median scores corresponded between the two clusters. Section 2 included nine five-point Likert-scale inquiries. The scoring measures were as; 2 = "Agree", 1 = "Somewhat Agree", 0 = neither “Neither Agree nor Disagree," -1 = "Somewhat Disagree," and, -2 = "Disagree". Participants were also asked to selfrate their knowledge of ChatGPT on a three-point Likert scale, with the options; -1 (’I have no idea’), 0 (’I have heard of it but cannot explain it’), and 1 (’I know the term and can explain it’). Group definitions were based on these self-ratings rather than direct knowledge demonstration. The median scores corresponded between the two clusters. Section 3 contained two multiple-choice inquiries, and the scoring measures were as: choosing the accurate response achieved a score of 1, while not assigning the accurate response resulted in a score of -1. All the data were gathered using Google Forms, and all the investigations were performed employing SPSS Version 26 software (IBM Corporation, New York, NY, USA). Quantitative data are shown as medians (IQR), while categorical data are reported as frequencies (percentages). Kolmogorov–Smirnov (K–S test) was utilized to test for the standard allocation of the constant variables before conducting inferential examinations. Mann-Whitney U was utilized to analyze the discrepancy in medians of the continuous variables (understanding of AI, the belief in ChatGPT, and literacy regarding the suitable usefulness of ChatGPT) between the targeted clusters. Pearson’s Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test investigated the relationship between categorical variables.

## Respondents’ survey

The Cronbach’s alpha values were 0.672, 0.814, and 0.742 for knowledge of AI, belief in ChatGPT, and literacy concerning the suitable usefulness of ChatGPT, respectively. A total count of 413 students, spread over five years from year 2 to interns, took part in the survey, transcending the originally targeted sampling size of 300. Among the participants, females were prevalent, accounting for 276 students (66.8%), while males accounted for 137 students (33.2%). Concerning their experiences with ChatGPT, 92 students (22.3%) reported using it, while 321 participants (77.7%) declared having never used the program. When questioned if they had heard of ChatGPT, 157 participants (38.0%) expressed they could define it, while 256 participants (62.0%) noted they had only heard of it, though they could not deliver a definition. ## Perceptions of ChatGPT output

The study collected the initial impressions of participants regarding ChatGPT. When participants were asked about ChatGPT’s reliability, 70.5% responded with "reliable" or "somewhat reliable." Likewise, when asked if ChatGPT helps them collect data in a better manner and efficiently, 84.5% of participants replied with "agree" or "somewhat agree." Further, in terms of comprehending their study conclusions, 75.6% of participants revealed that they "agree" or "somewhat agree" and that employed that ChatGPT improved their understanding. Moreover, 78.4% of the participants described that ChatGPT enhanced their educational efficiency, while 78.6% noted that it improved their understanding and acquaintance. However, regarding worries or distrust about utilizing ChatGPT, 52.3% of participants held reservations. Subsequently, the effect of ChatGPT on instruction and career was investigated. When questioned if they thought ChatGPT would thoroughly affect instructions, 78.0% of participants approved or somewhat approved, while 15.0% stayed unbiased. Concerning the sufficiency of answers and data delivered by ChatGPT for collecting data on pharmacy-related topics, only 57.7% revealed that they were acceptable or somewhat acceptable. Likewise, when participants were asked if they thought ChatGPT would help pharmacists enhance their career efficiency, 70.2% answered that it would be beneficial or somewhat beneficial, with 21.1% opting for neutrality (Table 3).

## Discussion

## Current status of ChatGPT use

According to a previous study, ChatGPT has shown great promise as a reliable educational tool. It achieved an accuracy rate of 79% in answering 264 clinical pharmacy-related multiple-choice questions. The study also found that ChatGPT’s answers and justifications had a 95% concordance rate, with high reproducibility within and between days.<sup>14</sup>

Although the benefits of integrating ChatGPT into educational frameworks are recognized, our study indicates low literacy among pharmacy students surveyed. This is observed from a low percentage of correct responses to all inappropriate (1.2%) and appropriate uses (2.7%). This reflects a significant gap in understanding ethical issues and the proper usage of ChatGPT. Furthermore, the analysis showed no significant difference in the overall ethical literacy scores between the two awareness groups, indicating a general deficiency in the comprehensive ethical understanding of ChatGPT. Additionally, a considerable portion of participants (52.3%) expressed concerns or uncertainties regarding using ChatGPT.

The findings from this study provide critical insights into the knowledge and ethical awareness of ChatGPT usage among pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia. The study reveals that a significant portion of pharmacy students (62.55%) lack awareness about AI concepts, which indicates a potential area for educational improvement. This lack of awareness matters because a solid understanding of ChatGPT is essential for future pharmacists who will increasingly rely on AI tools in clinical decision-making, patient care, and pharmacy management. Therefore, enhancing the application of AI pharmacy education is imperative to ensure that students are adequately prepared to integrate these tools into their professional practice responsibly and ethically, ultimately leading to improved healthcare outcomes.<sup>25,26</sup>

These findings align with previous research that identified significant gaps in AI education among medical students.<sup>25</sup> Interestingly, students with experience using ChatGPT demonstrated a greater familiarity with AI concepts, as illustrated in Table 1. The highest level of awareness was observed among pharmacy interns (48.5%), which suggest that students gain more knowledge about AI as they progress through their studies and gain practical experience. However, this difference was not statistically significant compared to other academic levels.<sup>27</sup>

Pharmacy students recognize the benefits of integrating ChatGPT into educational frameworks to improve their ability to interpret complex information, a vital skill in pharmacy education. ChatGPT can enhance academic performance by enhancing students’ abilit to understand and synthesize information effectively. Most participants (78.0%) believe ChatGPT would positively impact pharmacy education. This perception aligns with the views of healthcare workers in Saudi Arabia, who also consider ChatGPT as beneficial for the future of the healthcare system.<sup>28</sup> AI-powered adaptive learning platforms can customize education by providing immediate and accurate feedback.<sup>27,28</sup> However, our study indicates that only 57.7% of the participants found the information and responses provided by ChatGPT on pharmacy-related topics adequate or somewhat adequate.<sup>29</sup> In contrast, 75.1% of healthcare workers in Saudi Arabia reported feeling comfortable incorporating ChatGPT into their practice. This suggests that healthcare workers may have more experience with the practical applications and limitations of AI than pharmacy students.<sup>29</sup>

## Shortcomings of current ChatGPT use

Students needed help accurately describing general Al concepts, such as machine learning and deep learning. This issue may be addressed by integrating ChatGPT into the pharmacy curriculum and providing engaging educational workshops and training on basic pharmaceutical sciences and clinical applications. This approach will make learning more interactive and practical, accompanied by clear guidelines to ensure ethical use. In addition, it will help address the language barrier by offering Arabic language support. It will support the students, instructors, and preceptors. Additionally, the absence of well-defined policies for ChatGPT use impacts its adoption and usefulness. There is a need to map out the acceptable and unacceptable practices in ChatGPT domains. Unfamiliarity is another barrier among the students, which can be cleared by workshops and training.

This study has strengths and limitations to consider when interpreting the findings. This is the first study specifically designed to explore the knowledge and ethical awareness of using ChatGPT among pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia. The study involved participants from 23 pharmacy schools, representing a diverse student population. The rigorous methodology, employing stratified random sampling and validated survey instruments, adds to the study’s credibility and accuracy. However, the study relied on selfreported data, which may introduce the possibility of response bias as participants may overestimate or underestimate their knowledge and ethical awareness, which can affect the accuracy of the study’s findings.

## Standards of ethics

The ethical constraints of the ChatGPT, including its suitable uses and intrinsic shortcomings, were not well understood by the students. While 2.7% of all the students were able to identify all the proper items, only 1.2% of students could recognize the improper strains. To guarantee informed and responsible ChatGPT use in academic and professional context, several international institutions are currently seeking to put comprehensive guidelines, which is warranted by a lack of proper guidelines with regard to the ethical and legal requirements of the use of ChatGPT, and this needs to be supported by the improvements in literacy levels and objectives for the ChatGPT use with further modification on the existing trends and literacy levels. In the context of academics, a sense of moralistic use of ChatGPT exists all the time. Using the techniques also needs consistency with the desired priorities and emerging trends to support the development positively and honestly through ChatGPT. The clarity of the methodology, acceptance of the net resources, including its limitations in circumventing the information and source, ejection of inaccurate and biased outputs as compared against the credible source(s), violations of rights and privacy of individuals, and groups and organizations as well as institutions, and plagiarism, copyright infringement, and other forms of cheating, including not reporting the use of ChatGPT, and national, racial, and gender stereotyping, the inclusion of false information, and denial of credits, are also other ethical considerations while using the AI techniques and primarily ChatGPT. Maintaining academic integrity while using programs, especially AI-based ones, is crucial. ChatGPT, which offers added advantages, comes under the purview of ethical considerations of technology and software use. While using ChatGPT, students need to be open about it and provide rightful credit to the content creator. Radboud University’s policy demonstrated that using the text and code (s) generated by ChatGPT in an examination without disclosure constituted plagiarism.<sup>30</sup> Promoting a culture of independent study and a strong emphasis on academic integrity are necessary to prevent plagiarism. It might be challenging to strike this balance. Still, it can be fixed by adding more tests and using plagiarism detectors to ensure students’ work is original and follows the ethics of working and reporting the results. Therefore, a comprehensive educational strategy is essential to bridge the knowledge and ethical awareness gaps in ChatGPT usage among pharmacy students. The survey results advocated filling these gaps in pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia through institutional participation and guidelines development. Incorporating specific AI-based tools and courses, including ChatGPT, into the pharmacy curriculum will certainly help students gain a better grasp of these technologies, with an emphasis on their applications in pharmacy and other inter-related healthcare domains with the awareness of involvement of ethical implications in their use. In addition, interactive workshops need to be established to provide hands-on experience with ChatGPT through actual case studies that draw attention to the promise and limitations of such technology use. Furthermore, developing a strong ethical foundation inside educational institutions is also essential and will provide the exposure, perception and knowledge of the existence of moral use of technologies, as developing as ChatGPT. ## Current use status and developing ethical guidelines

The use of ChatGPT and other generative AI technological tools in academic contexts is still developing. While some schools have forbidden ChatGPT, others still need to have such restrictions, according to their feasibility, in place. On the other hand, severa organizations are proactively creating rules and regulations to deal with these technologies’ morality and appropriate uses. For instance, instructors at Champlain College and the University of Arizona, US, have restrictions regarding the use of ChatGPT for inclass assignments. Similarly, ChatGPT and other generative AI technologies can only be used for report writing with faculty clearance, according to preliminary rules released by Harvard University, and the University of Texas at Austin, both in the US. Students are expected to acknowledge the use of these technologies.<sup>31–34</sup> Using best practices for utilizing ChatGPT in higher educational contexts, the University of Pretoria, South Africa, has developed multiple recommendations. There is a more thorough approach. In addition to addressing the ethical issues surrounding such technology, these principles seek to improve teaching and learning through AI tools.<sup>35,36</sup> Additionally, the University of California system in the US is making efforts to offer guidelines for the appropriate applications of generative AI technologies. The intention is to foster creativity and investigation into these technologies while ensuring their applications don’t jeopardize confidential data and transgress the license agreements set out by the tool suppliers.<sup>37</sup> Most importantly, concerned colleges are proactively creating regulations and standards to guarantee the correct, ethical, and moral use of generative AI technologies, including ChatGPT, in academic contexts as the field continues to advance. The exercise is expected to fill the voids in knowledge, practices, and ethical guidelines to usher into an era of informed, conscientious, and utmost professional use of ChatGPT.<sup>38,39</sup>

## Conclusions

The study underlines the need for education on ChatGPT concepts and available AI generative tools, including ChatGPT. ChatGPT’s prospects, concerns, shortfalls, benefits, and concurrent use with the ethical requirements in pharmacy education, research, and professional development are outlined. ChatGPT and AI generative tools have broader roles as improvers and add-in tools towards achieving larger applications in pharmaceutical education’s improvement and pharmacy professionals’ outreach status to practice pharmacy for the professional and interested individuals is discussed. The study also underlines the need for improved and inclusive education on ChatGPT concepts in pharmacy curricula as well as ethical practice guidelines development in the pharmacy domains, which circumvents the other AI tools available and under development. The study additionally found that students who had utilized ChatGPT had higher levels of awareness in the pharmacy and inter-related health profession domains, showing that practical experiences boost knowledge and its outreach. Nonetheless, ChatGPT can potentially improve educational outcomes and enhance the professional knowledge base and experience for future work. Notwithstanding, several students also voiced reservations about its proper and ethical implementation, highlighting the need for explicit standards and rigorous ChatGPT and AI tools training with the professional and moral requirements of clear, focused and direct use. As a result, bridging the educational gaps in knowledge, perception, use and ethical tone is critical to students. The information and skills they need to utilize ChatGPT and other generative AI tools ethically to implement them effectively in their lives as pharmacy professionals are highly warranted.

The study acknowledges some of the limitations that could affect how the findings are interpreted. First, because of varying degrees of exposure and comprehension, it is challenging to compare the perceptions of users (Group 1) and non-users (Group 2) regarding ChatGPT. The majority of respondents represented in Group 2, might give answers that are more predicated on conjecture than wellinformed judgments, which would reduce the validity of their observations. Furthermore, using self-reported data raises the possibility of biases because it might not fully reflect participants’ objective understanding of ChatGPT emphasizing the need for additional study that includes more impartial evaluations.
