# Assessing the influence of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) on awareness and behavior in medical research integrity: An online survey study

Xiaoting Peng<sup>a,b\*</sup>, Yufeng Cai<sup>b\*</sup>, Dehua Hu<sup>b</sup>, Yi Guo<sup>b</sup>, Haixia Liu<sup>b</sup>, Xusheng Wu<sup>c</sup>, and Qingyuan Hu<sup>a</sup>

<sup>a</sup>Department of Scientific Research, The Third Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, Changsha, China; <sup>b</sup>School of Life Sciences, Central South University, Changsha, China; <sup>c</sup>Department of Scientific Research, Shenzhen Health Development Research and Data Management Center, Shenzhen, China

## ABSTRACT

Background: Generative Artificial Intelligence(GenAI) significantly enhances medical research efficiency but raises ethical concerns regarding research integrity. The lack of systematic guidelines for its ethical use underscores the need to investigate GenAI’s impact on researchers’ awareness and behavior concerning integrity.

Methods/Materials: A cross-sectional survey of 718 valid responses from Chinese medical researchers assessed GenAI’s impact on research integrity using an extended Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology(UTAUT) model.

Results: The findings reveal that performance expectancy, effort expectancy, technical environment, trust in technology, and supporting conditions positively influence researchers’ awareness of research integrity. Conversely, GenAI anxiety and perceived risks exert a significant negative impact. Furthermore, both supporting conditions and integrity awareness are positively associated with integrity behavior, while GenAI anxiety negatively affects such behavior.

Conclusion: The stakeholders in the medical research ecosystem should develop comprehensive guidelines for the responsible use of GenAI. Emphasis should be placed on optimizing the technical environment, enhancing trust and support structures, and embedding integrity safeguards, thereby promoting the synergistic development of technological innovation and ethical research practices.

## 1. Introduction

## 1.1. Social background: Emerging challenges to medical research integrity

Compared to other disciplines, medical research demands higher ethical standards and carries greater social responsibility, as it is directly tied to human life and health (F. Wang, Hou, and Zhang 2025). Any breach of integrity in this field can lead to misleading medical conclusions – undermining academic credibility and, more critically, posing significant risks to patient safety (Haitao, Xufeng, and Wen 2025). In recent years, there has been a noticeable global increase in the retraction of medical papers, with several hospitals and medical schools becoming embroiled in misconduct scandals that have triggered crises of public trust (Hua 2025; Noorden 2025). Thus, the integrity of medical research extends beyond compliance with academic norms (Ivan David et al. 2025); it represents a fundamental pillar for safeguarding public health, maintaining scientific credibility, and advancing the quality of healthcare.

## 1.2. Technical background: GenAI is rapidly penetrating the entire medical research lifecycle

GenAI employs neural networks and deep learning algorithms to produce coherent and logically structured content across multiple modalities – including audio, code, images, text, simulations, and video – based on the data it has been trained on (Company 2024). Within the realm of medical research, GenAI exhibits considerable potential, particularly in text generation and data processing (Jérémie and David 2024), offering novel methodological tools that can significantly enhance research efficiency and innovation (Alisha et al. 2024; Tanatorn et al. 2024; Xufei et al. 2025). Prior studies have employed GenAI to enhance the accuracy of medical image analysis and assisted adaptive cancer therapy (Tanatorn et al. 2025; Vishwanatha et al. 2025; Youcef 2025).

Currently, GenAI is being rapidly adopted across a wide array of sectors, including education (Y. Guoming, Li, and Teng 2024; H. Li 2023), journalism and media (Y. U. Guoming and Jianwei 2023), visual arts (Xiong 2017), finance (Dwivedi et al. 2023), and hospitality (Dwivedi et al. 2024), fundamentally transforming traditional models of knowledge production and dissemination. Studies have shown that GenAI fosters pedagogical innovation in higher education (Chiu et al. 2023), and improves the efficiency and impact of media and publishing (Lu 2023). It has received widespread acclaim from leading authorities such as MIT Technology Review, Gartner, and Nature (Groombridge et al. 2021, Nature 2023). Nevertheless, the powerful generative capabilities of GenAI have also sparked growing concerns, including issues related to data privacy, disinformation, algorithmic and racial biases, ethical risks, and heightened levels of AI-induced anxiety (Bauchner, Steinbrook, and Redberg 2025; Monteith et al. 2024; Tang 2023; Xie and Li 2023).

As GenAI increasingly integrates into all phases of medical research, it becomes imperative to investigate its influence on the integrity of scientific practices (Kayte 2025). In particular, there is a growing need to understand how GenAI may affect medical researchers’ cognitive judgments and behavioral choices related to research integrity. This study employs an empirical research approach to investigate the proposed questions in a systematic and data-driven manner.

While constructs such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, facilitating conditions, and social influence have been extensively examined and validated within the unified theory of acceptance and use of technology (UTAUT), the emergence of technologies like GenAI introduces additional factors that may influence medical researchers’ behavioral intentions. Accordingly, this study extends the UTAUT framework by incorporating the dimensions of technical trust, GenAI-anxiety and GenAI-perceived risk. Technical trust refers to the extent to which an individual perceives a given technology as trustworthy, reliable, and secure (Chen, Fan, and Azam 2024). GenAI-Perceived risk is the expectation of potential problems following the introduction of GenAI technology (M. C. Lee 2009). GenAI-Anxiety refers to apprehension and fear of using new and unfamiliar GenAI technologies (Budhathoki et al. 2024). This study aims to systematically investigate the attitudes and adoption mechanisms of medical researchers toward GenAI in the context of research integrity. It seeks to deepen the understanding of how GenAI influences researchers’ awareness and behaviors related to research integrity, thereby contributing to the sustainable development of this emerging field. By employing structural equation modeling (SEM), this study incorporates constructs such as performance expectations, effort expectation, supporting conditions, technical environment, trust, anxiety, and perceived risk into the research framework. The findings are expected to offer novel theoretical insights and practical implications for promoting research integrity.

The following chapters are organized as follows: we first review the existing literature on GenAI and medical research integrity, followed by a detailed description of the research methodology and data analysis procedures. We then present and discuss the empirical findings, and conclude with a summary of the key results and implications, along with suggestions for future research. Through this work, we aim to enhance the understanding of medical researchers’ attitudes toward GenAI-assisted research and their intentions to engage in integrity behaviors. This study provides both a theoretical foundation and practical guidance for the responsible integration of GenAI into the medical research landscape. Moreover, by incorporating the constructs of technical trust, GenAI-anxiety, and GenAIperceived risk into the research framework, we offer actionable recommendations for medical institutions seeking to improve and optimize the ethical and effective use of GenAI technologies.

## 2. Theoretical support and research hypotheses

## 2.1. Variables under the unified theory of acceptance and use of technology (UTAUT)

The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), proposed by Venkatesh(2003) (Venkatesh et al. 2003), is a comprehensive framework developed by integrating several prominent models of technology acceptance. These include the Theory of Reasoned Action, the Technology Acceptance Model, the Motivational Model, the Theory of Planned Behavior, the Innovation Diffusion Theory, and Social Cognitive Theory (Williams, Rana, and Dwivedi 2015). The UTAUT model (Figure 1) is designed to predict user acceptance and use of technology by accounting for multiple influencing factors and individual differences. It identifies four core constructs – performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions – along with four moderating variables: gender, age, experience, and voluntariness of use (Venkatesh, Thong, and Xu 2016).

UTAUT has been widely employed by researchers both domestically and internationally to investigate user acceptance and behavior across various technological contexts. For example, performance expectancy and effort expectancy significantly influenced users’ willingness to adopt chatbot services (Balakrishnan, Abed, and Jones 2022), while social influence and facilitating conditions either promoted or hindered the adoption of GenAI in new product development teams (Xia and Chen 2024). Some studies have also applied the UTAUT model to evaluate the adoption of ChatGPT in medical education (Noura et al. 2025), understanding older adults’ intention to use telehealth (Anne, Joneen, and Kristen 2025), and examine students’ and teachers’ acceptance and use of educational technologies (Xue, Rashid, and Sha 2024).

## 2.1.1. Performance expectations (PE)

In this study, performance expectations refer to medical researchers’ subjective perceptions of the extent to which GenAI can enhance research efficiency and improve the quality of research outputs. Research shows that performance expectations significantly influence users’ evaluation of generative artificial intelligence (Jiayu et al. 2025; Qingran 2025). When researchers perceive GenAI as a valuable tool for optimizing research workflows and producing higher-quality outcomes, they are more likely to adopt the technology in a responsible and compliant manner. This responsible use, in turn, may reinforce their awareness of research integrity. Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H1: In medical research activities, higher performance expectations regarding GenAI are significantly associated with increased awareness of research integrity among medical researchers.

## 2.1.2. Effort expectation (EE)

In this study, effort expectation refers to medical researchers’ perceptions of the operational ease, learning cost, and usability burden associated with GenAI technologies. It reflects their subjective assessment of how easy GenAI is to learn and use in the context of research practice. When researchers perceive GenAI as intuitive and easy to integrate into their workflows, they are more inclined to invest effort in understanding its responsible application and to engage more consciously with research integrity norms – viewing them as essential to ensuring research credibility. Conversely, if GenAI is perceived as complex, time-consuming, or difficult to navigate, researchers may prioritize convenience over compliance, potentially overlooking ethical requirements and increasing the likelihood of misconduct. Research shows that effort expectancy significantly positively affect behavioral intention (Jiayu et al. 2025). Thus, greater perceived ease of use is expected to enhance researchers’ awareness of research integrity when adopting GenAI. Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H2: In medical research activities, a higher level of effort expectation is significantly associated with greater awareness of research integrity among medical researchers.

## 2.1.3. Technical environment (TE)

In the context of medical research, the broader technical environment encompasses national regulatory frameworks, editorial policies from academic publishers, and institutional management systems that collectively shape the compliant use of GenAI technologies. Unlike the broader construct of “social influence” the technical environment more precisely captures the external structural factors that directly govern how medical researchers utilize GenAI in practice. Social influence significantly affect learners’ behavioral intention to use GenAI (Qingran 2025). For example, national policies define the overarching regulatory boundaries for AI usage; journal publication guidelines dictate how AI assistance must be disclosed in manuscripts; and internal rules within medical institutions regulate the permissible use of GenAI in scientific workflows.

This study defines the technical environment as the constellation of external support systems – such as policy directives, publication standards, and institutional protocols – that guide researchers in the ethical and appropriate integration of GenAI. A clearly articulated regulatory infrastructure can reduce ambiguity, prevent misuse, and reinforce adherence to research integrity norms. In contrast, vague or inconsistent external guidance may lead to confusion, potentially fostering misconduct such as plagiarism, improper attribution, or concealment of AI involvement. Therefore, a wellstructured technical environment serves as a critical external condition for enhancing researchers’ awareness of research integrity. Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H3: In medical research activities, a well-established technical environment is significantly associated with greater awareness of research integrity among medical researchers.

## 2.1.4. Supporting conditions (SC)

In medical research contexts, technology adoption by researchers is influenced not only by individual perceptions but also by objective factors such as hardware infrastructure, user interfaces, and network environments. To more accurately capture these real-world constraints, this study replaces UTAUT’s original “facilitating conditions” with the concept of supporting conditions. We define supporting conditions as the availability of comprehensive infrastructure and technical support that directly enable researchers to use GenAI. Study shows that facilitating conditions positively influence the behavioral intention to use GenAI tools (Dominika, Cintia, and Ana Isabel 2025).

When supporting conditions are robust, researchers face lower operational burdens and are more willing to adopt GenAI in a compliant manner, thereby fostering greater awareness of research integrity. Moreover, adequate technical support ensures that researchers can execute workflows according to established norms, reducing the likelihood of misconduct arising from technological obstacles and enhancing their actual integrity behaviors. Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H6: In medical research activities, strong supporting conditions are significantly associated with higher levels of research integrity awareness among medical researchers.

H10: In medical research activities, strong supporting conditions are significantly associated with improved research integrity behavior among medical researchers.

## 2.2. Technical trust, GenAI-Anxiety and GenAI-Perceived risk

GenAI built upon large language models (LLMs) and deep learning technologies, possesses powerful content generation capabilities and is rapidly reshaping the entire medical research lifecycle. Prior studies have demonstrated that GenAI significantly influences multiple stages of the scientific research process, including data collection, statistical analysis, figure generation, manuscript writing, language editing, citation management, authorship attribution, peer review, and even manuscript submission and publication workflows (Cooper 2023; P. Wang and Xu 2023). Specifically, GenAI can support researchers in creative ideation (Bouschery, Blazevic, and Piller 2023), scientific writing (Silva et al. 2023), and the organization and analysis of research data (Blandchand et al. 2023), substantially improving research productivity and the overall quality of scientific output.

The widespread integration of GenAI into academic research has prompted swift policy responses from major international academic publishers. Leading journals such as Science, The Lancet, and Cell have publicly stated that ChatGPT and similar AI tools cannot be credited as authors, though their use as research assistants is permitted. Key regulatory bodies – including the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE), the World Association of Medical Editors (WAME), and the Council of

Science Editors (CSE) – have issued formal guidelines for the responsible use of AI-generated content. For instance, Nature has published specific editorial recommendations on the application of ChatGPT in academic writing and peer review, aiming to define ethical boundaries and acceptable usage practices (Gruda 2024). Current literature indicates that GenAI has a dual impact on the integrity of medical research, offering both potential benefits and emerging risks. On the positive side, GenAI can facilitate paradigm shifts in research methodologies (Ruksakulpiwat, Kumar, and Ajibade 2023), assist in disease data screening (Cao and Liu 2022), and enhance research efficiency (P. Y. Lee et al. 2023). Conversely, concerns have been raised about reduced credibility in AI-assisted research findings (Saini and Saxena 2023), compromised publication integrity (Rahimi and Abadi 2023), ambiguous intellectual property ownership (Jeyaraman et al. 2023), and the absence of wellestablished ethical norms.

As the technology continues to evolve, domain-specific GenAI models tailored for medical applications have emerged. Examples include BioMedLM 2.7B, LLaMA (Large Language Model Meta AI), Bio-BERT, Bio-NLP, Med-PaLM 2, Bio-Megatron, Clinical-BERT, XGen-7B, GatorTronGPT, Chat-Doctor, and others (Cascella et al. 2024; H. Guo et al. 2024). These models demonstrate specialized capabilities such as comprehension of medical terminology, integration of interdisciplinary knowledge, and interpretation of clinical language. They have been applied to a wide range of tasks, including dental research (Liwen and Yang 2024), drug development pipelines (Y. Li 2023), mining of drug – disease associations (Gao et al. 2024), and optimization of cancer screening and treatment protocols (Iannantuono et al. 2023). Moreover, GenAI is increasingly used to support clinicians by providing multidimensional information, aiding clinical decision-making, and enhancing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies (Thirunavukarasu et al. 2023; Walker et al. 2023; F. Wang 2023). In areas such as pathology and clinical imaging, AI systems have shown high levels of accuracy and reliability in image recognition, automated diagnostics, and report generation (Rau et al. 2023; Schukow et al. 2024). These technologies can also help patients better understand test results, fostering greater trust and engagement in medical decision-making processes (Cadamuro et al. 2023).

## 2.2.1. GenAI-Perceived risk (GenAI-PR)

Perceived risk has been widely studied in areas such as e-commerce (Lin et al. 2025), sports lottery purchasing behavior (Özkan A, Begum, and Murat 2025), and acceptance of GenAI (Sirui and Yuhong 2025). In this study, GenAI-perceived risk refers to the subjective assessment by medical researchers of the potential threats and negative consequences associated with using GenAI in scientific research activities. These perceived risks include concerns over data privacy breaches, the dissemination of misinformation, violations of ethical norms, and the possible misuse or misinterpretation of AIgenerated outputs. When medical researchers perceive high levels of uncertainty or ethical risk associated with GenAI, they may – whether consciously or unconsciously – engage in nonstandard research behaviors in an attempt to avoid potential pitfalls or to secure desired outcomes. This may include actions such as concealing AI assistance, bypassing review protocols, or downplaying the limitations of AI-generated data. Over time, such practices can erode researchers’ commitment to upholding scientific integrity. Moreover, heightened risk perception may foster distrust toward GenAI technologies, resulting in anxiety about proper usage and diminishing adherence to research ethics guidelines. Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H4: In medical research activities, higher levels of GenAI-perceived risk are significantly associated with lower awareness of research integrity among medical researchers.

## 2.2.2. Technical trust (TT)

Previous research has identified technical trust as a critical determinant of artificial intelligence adoption (Cheng et al. 2022; Tanantong, Nantajeewarawat, and Thiemjarus 2016). In social science research, technical trust has been applied as a key variable in studies on smart home healthcare services and autonomous driving technologies (Shugang et al. 2023; Wieland, Michael, and Marek 2025). In this study, technical trust is defined as medical researchers’ perceptions of GenAI’s understandability, reliability, and interpretability. It captures researchers’ confidence in the accuracy, transparency, and verifiability of AI-generated outputs. When researchers hold a high level of technical trust in GenAI, they are more likely to explicitly disclose its use, rigorously validate its outputs, and adhere to established research-integrity standards. Such trust encourages rational engagement with GenAI – valuing not only efficiency but also the authenticity and traceability of research data – thereby reinforcing integrity awareness. Conversely, insufficient trust may lead researchers to conceal AI usage to avoid scrutiny, undermining their commitment to ethical research practices. Based on this reasoning, we propose:

H5: In medical research activities, higher technical trust in GenAI is significantly associated with greater awareness of research integrity among medical researchers.

## 2.2.3. GenAI anxiety (GenAI-AY)

Previous research has applied the concept of AI anxiety to areas such as unemployment-related anxiety and knowledge payment systems, with some studies indicating that cognitive characteristics may alleviate anxiety associated with artificial intelligence (Jinsong, Miao, and Jinhua 2025; Mehmet, $\mathrm { { C } , }$ and Muhammet Faruk 2024; Xintong and Yipeng 2025). GenAI anxiety refers to the apprehension and unease that medical researchers may experience when interacting with GenAI tools in the course of their scientific work (Budhathoki et al. 2024). Such anxiety can undermine researchers’ focus on integrity norms, diminish their willingness to adhere to ethical standards, and impair their judgment regarding research ethics. Moreover, heightened GenAI anxiety may prompt avoidance or maladaptive coping strategies, increasing the risk of engaging in misconduct such as plagiarism or data fabrication. Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H7: In medical research activities, higher levels of GenAI anxiety are significantly associated with lower awareness of research integrity among medical researchers.

H9: In medical research activities, higher levels of GenAI anxiety are significantly associated with reduced willingness to adhere to research integrity.

## 2.3. Medical research integrity awareness (MRIA)

Research on the characteristics of retracted papers in the medical field has primarily focused on factors such as the reasons for retraction, time to retraction, journal impact factor, number of retracted articles, authorship patterns, involved journals, and disciplinary domains (Kocyigit, Akyol, et al. 2023). Common reasons for retraction include data fabrication or falsification (Deng and Wu 2021; Ren, Jin, and Weng 2019), duplicate publication (Jin, Liao, and Zhou 2023), and violations of ethical standards (Sun, Wang, and Ding 2023). For instance, 41.8% of retracted medical papers were withdrawn due to fraudulent peer review practices (Kocyigit, Zhaksylyk, et al. 2023). Additional causes include third-party authorship or submission services, commercialization of manuscripts (Y. Jiang, Liu, and Sun 2022), fabrication of the research process, and manipulation or falsification of research data (Y. Jiang, Liu, and Sun 2022; Kaibing, Xu, and Jue 2023).

Some studies have conducted in-depth investigations into prominent academic misconduct events. Notable examples include repeated publications by a single research group at a major university (Cui, Zhao, and Zheng 2018), the high-profile “Chen Jin Incident” in China (M. Jiang, Jiang, and Duan 2013), the mass retraction of 117 papers authored by Chinese scholars by an international publishing group in 2015 (Li and Wang 2019), and the retraction of 107 papers by the journal Tumor Biology in 2017 (X. Li, Chen, and Guan 2022). These events have had a profound negative impact on the reputation of the associated institutions and disciplines, and have significantly damaged the international credibility of academic research originating from the country (Wu et al. 2022). Among the various forms of misconduct, violations of ethical norms – particularly in clinical and animal research – have emerged as especially prominent in the medical sciences (Ma 2022).

In this study, medical research integrity awareness refers to the intrinsic disposition or attitudinal orientation of medical researchers toward adhering to ethical research norms and preserving the authenticity and objectivity of scientific inquiry when utilizing GenAI-assisted methods. This construct reflects the degree to which researchers recognize and value the principles of research integrity and constitutes a foundational element of their ethical cognition. As a key internal cognitive driver, research integrity awareness fosters a proactive commitment to normative conduct and reduces the likelihood of research misconduct. Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H8: In medical research activities, higher awareness of research integrity significantly promotes compliance with research integrity behaviors among medical researchers.

To address this gap, the present study extends the original UTAUT framework by incorporating variables relevant to the unique characteristics of GenAI within medical research workflows. Specifically, we introduce GenAI perceived risk, technology trust, and GenAI anxiety to capture the psychological and ethical challenges that such emerging technologies may pose. This adapted model is employed to analyze the key factors influencing medical researchers’ awareness and behavior concerning research integrity.

## 3.1. Research design, Methodology, and data collection technique

This study employed a quantitative approach to examine causal relationships between GenAI and medical research integrity. A cross-sectional survey design was adopted, targeting individuals involved in medical research across

Data collection occurred between 30 November 2024, and 20 January 2025. Eight hundred survey invitations containing a URL link were distributed to researchers randomly selected from various medical institutions. To increase the questionnaire completion rate, brief motivational messages were embedded at key points (e.g., “One-third of the questionnaire has been completed – thank you for your participation!”; “Twothirds of the questionnaire has been completed – your effort is greatly appreciated!”; “Thank you for completing the questionnaire – your contribution is outstanding!”). In total, 718 valid responses were obtained, resulting in a response rate of approximately 89.8%.

Prior to full-scale distribution, a pilot study was conducted to assess and refine the questionnaire. Based on feedback from the pretest, several items were modified to enhance clarity and to ensure the reliability and validity of the measurement model examining GenAI’s impact on medical research integrity.

## 3.2. Measurements and analysis tool

The questionnaire comprised 69 items designed to measure nine constructs: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, technical environment, GenAIperceived risk, technical trust, supporting conditions, GenAI anxiety, awareness of medical research integrity, and research integrity behavior. All items were adapted from previously validated instruments and reworded to reflect the context of GenAI’s influence on medical research integrity. Specifically, the scales for medical research integrity awareness and medical research integrity behavior were derived from: the “Notice on Medical Research Integrity and Related Behavioral Norms” (National Health Commission of the PRC, 2021) (China N H C o t P s R o 2021); the “Boundary Guidelines for the Use of AIGC in Academic Publishing” (Institute of Scientific and Technical Information of China, 2023) (China I o S a T I o 2024); and the “Manual of Research Integrity Norms” (National Natural Science Foundation of China, 2023) (China N N s F o 2023). Respondents rated all items on a seven-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 7 (“strongly agree”). Data were analyzed using covariance-based structural equation modeling (SEM) to assess both the measurement model and the hypothesized structural relationships. ## 5.1. Discussion

## 5.1.1. Technical drivers enhancing integrity awareness

This study demonstrates that performance expectancy, effort expectancy, technical environment, trust in technology, and supporting conditions are each positively associated with medical research integrity awareness, which exhibits a degree of concordance with the conclusions by Bouteraa (Mohamed et al. 2024). These findings reveal the underlying mechanisms through which technical factors influence researchers’ perceptions of integrity. Specifically, performance expectancy leads medical researchers to view GenAI as a valuable tool for improving the quality and efficiency of research. In specialized domains – such as optimizing clinical diagnosis and treatment pathways or exploring disease-drug mechanisms – the credibility of GenAI-assisted outcomes depends heavily on the authenticity of research data and the standardization of analytical procedures, thereby reinforcing integrity awareness. The positive impact of effort expectancy suggests that when researchers perceive GenAI as easy to learn and use, they are more inclined to incorporate it into their workflows in a manner consistent with ethical research norms. Improvements in the technical environment provide clear operational guidelines for GenAI-supported research, helping to prevent unintentional misconduct arising from regulatory ambiguity (Tanatorn, Ekawit, and Surapa 2015). Trust in technology further strengthens researchers’ confidence in GenAI outputs – particularly in contexts such as drug trials, medical imaging, and multimodal data analysis – thus mitigating unethical practices like data manipulation or “result beautification.” Additionally, robust supporting conditions increase the accessibility and usability of GenAI, fostering an environment conducive to the development of integrity-conscious research behaviors. Moving forward, medical institutions are encouraged to cultivate a research integrity framework centered on the progression from “technology acceptance” to “trust building” to “awareness internalization,” supported through system optimization, training and education, and technical infrastructure, thereby reinforcing ethical standards in the medical research ecosystem.

## 5.1.2. Psychological and ethical pressures undermining integrity

This study further indicates that perceived risks and anxiety related to GenAI are negatively associated with medical research integrity awareness, which provides empirical support for the scholarly view that ethical considerations and academic integrity can shape perceptions of the use and utility of GenAI (Metwaly Ali Mohamed, Ahmed Maher Khafaga, and Abdelrahman Saber Abdelrahman 2025). These findings highlight how the combined psychological and ethical pressures introduced by emerging technologies can erode researchers’ commitment to integrity. Medical research often involves extensive realworld data, particularly sensitive patient information. Researchers’ concerns about the opacity and lack of transparency in GenAI’s data processing mechanisms heighten perceived risks, which may lead some to limit data sharing or engage in nonstandard data manipulation – practices that compromise research integrity. Anxiety is especially prevalent among medical researchers operating in highly competitive environments, where professional advancement, project funding, and publication success are under constant pressure. Uncertainty about GenAI’s effectiveness, fears of technological displacement, and unclear boundaries surrounding its lawful use contribute to hesitation, discomfort, and even avoidance in its application. Such emotional and cognitive stressors undermine researchers’ ability to make objective judgments and adhere to ethical research practices. To address these challenges, medical research institutions should promote informed, rational, and compliant GenAI usage by implementing transparent risk communication strategies, clarifying usage norms, and offering targeted anxiety-reduction interventions. These efforts can help strengthen research integrity at its foundation.

## 5.1.3. From awareness to Action: Factors Influencing integrity behaviors

This study further demonstrates that awareness of medical research integrity and supporting conditions significantly promote integrity-related behaviors, whereas GenAI-related anxiety exerts a detrimental effect. Integrity awareness, as an internalized driver of normative behavior, encourages medical researchers to emphasize verification and compliance when utilizing GenAI-generated content, thereby reducing misconduct such as plagiarism, data fabrication, and unauthorized reuse driven by overreliance on the technology. In contrast, GenAI-induced anxiety can impair researchers’ judgment and diminish their willingness to adhere to ethical standards, especially under the pressure of performance evaluations and publication demands, increasing the risk of noncompliant use. To effectively standardize research behavior in the GenAI era, it is essential to strengthen integrity awareness, enhance institutional and technical support systems, and implement psychological interventions that mitigate anxiety and promote ethical decision-making.

## 5.2. Implications to theory and practice

The empirical findings derived from this study make substantive contributions to the advancement of theoretical frameworks and the enhancement of practical strategies. In terms of theoretical implications, this study grounded in the UTAUT model, integrates key psychological and cognitive variables related to the use of generative artificial intelligence – namely GenAI perceived risk, technology trust, and GenAI anxiety – to systematically examine the mechanisms that influence medical researchers’ awareness of and adherence to research integrity. It expands the theoretical boundaries at the intersection of generative AI and research ethics. By incorporating research integrity as a behavioral outcome variable for the first time within the context of technology acceptance and use, this study highlights the critical role of both technical attitudes and emotional factors – such as AI-related anxiety – in shaping ethical research behavior. These findings enrich the theoretical framework of technology-related ethical behavior and provide valuable theoretical insights for constructing a model of technological ethics tailored to the medical research environment.

Concerning the practical implications, this study demonstrates that factors such as performance expectations, effort expectation, technical environment, and technology trust significantly enhance researchers’ awareness of research integrity and their compliance behaviors. Accordingly, strategies should be adopted for different stakeholders in medical research integrity. For employees, it is essential to proactively develop a rational understanding of and competence in using GenAI tools. Researchers should be adept at leveraging GenAI to improve research efficiency while maintaining a strong sense of responsibility for the content it generates. Moreover, they must remain vigilant against improper use stemming from technical anxiety or cognitive biases, strengthen self-discipline, and refrain from using AI-generated content for publication without adequate review, thus upholding the ethical standards of scientific research. For managers, attention should be paid to fluctuations in researchers trust in technology and anxiety during the use of GenAI. Effective psychological support and technical training mechanisms should be established. On the one hand, organizing targeted training sessions and experience-sharing initiatives can enhance researchers’ proficiency with and confidence in AI tools. On the other hand, risk identification and feedback channels should be implemented to help researchers recognize and mitigate potential ethical risks. In particular, managers must clearly define the boundary between AI-assisted and humangenerated content and develop usage guidelines and disclosure protocols to reduce the risk of research misconduct.

## 5.3. Limitations and future directions

This study elucidates the mechanism through which GenAI influences medical research integrity, offering a theoretical foundation for regulating AI applications and safeguarding research authenticity. Nonetheless, several limitations and opportunities for future research remain. First, our analysis did not differentiate among specific medical professions or disciplines. Future studies should examine how GenAI’s impact on research integrity varies across diverse research paradigms and specialty areas. Second, we did not explore the co-evolutionary dynamics between technological perceptions and research integrity within interdisciplinary contexts. Finally, our sample was limited by the accessibility of medical faculty, hospital researchers, and administrative personnel, which may introduce bias and constrain the generalizability of our findings. Future research could examine how evaluation systems across various disciplines address the risks associated with technological misuse from the perspectives of science, engineering, the humanities, and the social sciences. Additionally, cross-cultural and crosssectional studies involving medical researchers from diverse cultural and social backgrounds could be conducted to explore how national research integrity policies influence the role of generative artificial intelligence in advancing scientific development.
