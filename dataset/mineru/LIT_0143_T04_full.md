# Responsible AI usage for academic integrity in China

Yudi Fernando and Yu Li

Yudi Fernando is based at Faculty of Industrial Management, Universiti Malaysia Pahang Al-Sultan Abdullah, Kuantan, Malaysia. Yu Li is based at Faculty of Education and Liberal Studies, City University of Malaysia, Petaling Jaya, Malaysia, and Faculty of Education, Jiangxi Ganzhou Technician College, Ganzhou, China.

## Abstract

Purpose – This paper aims to explore how responsible artificial intelligence (AI) technology can enhance various aspects of academic achievement, benefiting institutions, teachers, students and society in creating productive and better educational systems.

Design/methodology/approach – Data were collected through interviews using convenience sampling involving students, university managers and lecturers. An inductive approach was applied among academic stakeholders in China.

Findings – The results show that students and lecturers use and emphasize AI technology in academics. Considering the responses, this study found that human-machine integration significantly improves the learning experience, including independent learning, better suggestions, ideas for solutions, module checking, curriculum setup and others. This study identifies the challenges associated with academic integrity and the unethical use of AI to enhance the learning experience and achieve better academic results.

Practical implications – The findings of this study help better understand the parameters that affect college students’ and educators’ confidence in and acceptance of AI systems. This paper has provided a theoretical foundation for developing AI systems that help students succeed academically and in various other learning contexts.

Originality/value – This paper has proposed a framework of responsible AI usage for academic integrity that can be used as the basis for understanding best practices. The study has identified the challenges and proposed a solution for the benefit of AI utilization in academia. AI technology is continuously developing, and its utilization will evolve in the future compared to how people are currently using it. In this study, the authors conclude that AI will certainly be widely used and could not be avoided. Educational stakeholders must adhere to clear standard guidelines and fairly assess AI and plagiarismrelated work using expert human judgment, free from personal bias. As AI continues to advance, instructors and students will need to develop skills in identifying, evaluating, and investigating various aspects of AI in academic contexts.

Keywords Artificial intelligence, Learning experience, Academic integrity, Academic dishonesty, Moral awareness

Paper type Research paper

## Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) is one of the most compelling aspects of how human existence has changed, even in education. AI, specifically computer systems, replicates human behavior and intelligence by machines (Telceken et al., 2025; Leal Filho et al., 2023). The current value of the worldwide AI market is \$142.3bn, and it is expected to continue to expand due to the influx of investments (Thormundsson, 2023). AI technologies have become increasingly prevalent in recent years, impacting academic performance and integrity. Incorporating AI and other technological innovations in educational settings has created several chances to enhance the learning experience, simplify administrative work and improve overall academic performance. According to Foroughi et al. (2023), AI will revolutionize learning and teaching in the future and will change the landscape of the educational system. However, other scholars have described the challenges of incorporating responsible AI into the educational system. The standardization of AI usage among academicians continues the debates (Akbar et al., 2023; Ng et al., 2023).

Along with the educational opportunities AI has brought forth, it also raises questions about students’ adherence to academic honesty. The term academic integrity refers to the standards of conduct that should be upheld by all academic community members, including professors, researchers and students (Boehm et al., 2009). Technology development and machine learning have completely changed educational environments and how students access information and acquire knowledge. Advanced information technology usage is supposed not to limit the exploration, innovation and integrity in teaching, learning and research, yet it will open new opportunities when it is used responsibly.

According to previous scholars, AI offers a significant opportunity to advance and revolutionize future teaching and research (Alhumaid et al., 2023). Artificially intelligent software such as Bard AI and ChatGPT converse with and understand human language, and they also produce content based on scholarly and student’s input (Mondal et al., 2023). On the other hand, the adoption of AI has been viewed as a threat by certain people or scholars because it may produce content, which means that students could potentially abuse it (Limna et al., 2022). Explaining how AI affects college students’ ethical behavior and academic performance is vital as educational technology becomes more widely used. Understanding the complex relationship between generative AI technology adoption and academic integrity compliance is essential for educational administrators, faculties and policymakers. Furthermore, exploring the potential drivers of responsible AI usage will shed light on how adopting advanced technology tools influences college students’ academic achievement and adherence to academic integrity.

There are several previous findings related to AI that reflect on its impact. However, it is difficult to determine to what extent AI blends and complies with academic integrity, especially among college students, which impacts their performance (Chan, 2023). It is also challenging to evaluate how AI affects academic users’ behavior, attitude, motivation for using it, academic integrity, academic achievements and the specific role institutes play in ensuring ethical standards in AI and educational integration. This study has contributed to thoroughly identifying these aspects.

This study has provided insights and a better understanding of the detailed aspects linked with AI integration in academics, which will help users save time, focus more on research topics and manage complexity. It will also help global universities understand the concept of integration, ethical follow-ups, their responsibilities and the setting of standard practices. Finally, it will benefit users in understanding the associated misconduct issues and how they can use AI reasonably. This study addresses two research questions:

RQ1. What specific factors drive the responsible conceptualization and practice of AI in the educational sector and how can these be standardized?

RQ2. How can the insights from university stakeholders be used to develop a comprehensive AI usage framework that addresses both ethical and practical challenges?

## Literature review

The integration of Industry 4.0 into educational practices has sparked discussions about the impact of AI on both ethical behavior and academic achievement. As AI becomes more prevalent in academia, several ethical considerations must be addressed to ensure its responsible and fair use. These concerns primarily revolve around data privacy and security, transparency and accountability and informed consent. Data privacy and security are critical when implementing AI systems in academia (Zeb et al., 2025). These systems rely heavily on vast amounts of data to function optimally. Universities must safeguard the privacy and security of student and faculty data, protecting sensitive information from unauthorized access and misuse. Transparency and accountability are also essential when using AI technologies in decision-making processes (Rezaei et al., 2024). Academic institutions must be transparent about the incorporation of AI systems and establish clear guidelines to uphold accountability for the outcomes generated by these systems.

Informed consent is another crucial aspect. Data collected and analyzed using AI technologies from students or faculty members requires informed consent (Hossain et al., 2025; Kalfeli and Angeli, 2025). It is recommended that individuals obtain informed consent, ensuring they are aware of how their data are used and have the choice to opt-out if desired. The ethical utilization of AI-generated content within universities can be delineated into multiple interconnected, overlapping and successive phases, including creation, dissemination, usage and evaluation. Each phase plays a crucial role in shaping the ethical landscape within academic institutions. AI tools, such as ChatGPT and Bard AI, offer significant advantages to university students in knowledge acquisition and information retrieval (Ahmed et al., 2024). These tools leverage advanced natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning algorithms to provide instant access to a vast pool of information and resources. They contribute to the development of essential skills such as problem-solving, creativity and adaptability, preparing students for the modern workforce (Elbanna and Armstrong, 2024). The integration of AI tools in academic settings can revolutionize learning, collaboration and academic success. AI tools can analyze individual learning patterns and preferences to tailor educational materials and resources to suit each student’s specific needs (Gligorea et al., 2023). In addition, AI-powered tools can create interactive simulations and immersive learning environments that engage students in hands-on learning experiences (Rapaka et al., 2025).

Al Ka’bi (2023) asserts that AI enhances student engagement by offering personalized instructional experiences and automating administrative processes. This active engagement enriches the learning process, facilitating the discovery of results and potential outcomes. AI algorithms streamline the assessment process by automatically grading assignments, providing detailed feedback and identifying areas where students may need additional support. Engaged students are more likely to participate in classroom activities, collaborate with peers, ask questions and seek clarification. These actions improve knowledge retention, sharpen critical thinking abilities and enhance overall academic performance. AI-generated content is not a novel concept in academia; 95% of students are familiar with tools like ChatGPT, and a portion of them use it regularly (Sto¨ hr et al., 2024). Despite this widespread use, many students lack awareness of the ethical rules and guidelines set forth by universities.

Lo (2024a) observes that users who subscribe to the premium version of AI-generated content exhibit higher levels of AI literacy compared to nonsubscribers. This suggests that access to advanced AI tools may contribute to a deeper understanding of AI technologies (Hornberger et al., 2025). However, in the realm of AI ethics, users encounter various challenges (Lo, 2024b). These challenges include issues related to transparency, bias and fairness, particularly among specific user groups. Transparency concerns revolve around understanding how AI algorithms make decisions and operate (Kovari, 2024), whereas bias and fairness issues pertain to ensuring that AI systems do not perpetuate or amplify discriminatory practices and treat all individuals equitably. Addressing these ethical challenges requires a comprehensive approach that includes developing and implementing clear guidelines for the ethical use of AI technologies and promoting transparency in AI systems. In addition, it involves mitigating bias through careful design and data practices and ensuring fairness in algorithmic outcomes across diverse user populations. By actively engaging with these challenges and implementing ethical frameworks, users can navigate the complexities of AI technology responsibly and ethically.

Vigdor et al. (2014) argued that students face crucial decisions about their future before graduation. Some students may opt to further their education by pursuing an advanced degree or exploring a different field of study. AI systems have the potential to offer valuable guidance to users based on their academic performance and interests in their intended field of study. The transition from academia to the professional world is a key aspiration. AI can play a significant role in assisting students during this critical juncture by providing career tools that align their aspirations with the pathways needed to achieve them. AI can provide students with real-time data and insights into job market trends, salary ranges, demand for specific skills and potential career growth opportunities. AI algorithms can analyze a student’s skills, interests and academic background to suggest suitable career paths that align with their strengths and aspirations (Bankins et al., 2024).

In addition, AI-powered career advisors can offer personalized guidance based on a college student’s background, experience, location preferences, abilities and professional aspirations. This tailored approach equips students with customized study tracks and potential opportunities for career advancement and growth. According to Hunter and Bowen (2023), IBM’s AI-driven Blue Matching system represents a promising job-matching service for the future. This system leverages AI to connect individuals with suitable job opportunities based on their skills, qualifications and preferences. For example, Elon University uses AI technology to assist students in reviewing past courses, applying acquired knowledge to course planning, receiving personalized feedback and engaging in tailored learning experiences (Hannan and Liu, 2023).

The integration of AI technology into the educational sector has led to a significant transformation, automating various processes and enhancing teaching and learning practices. AI has become a critical platform in education, driving improvements in personalized learning experiences and educational outcomes. Pataranutaporn et al. (2021) suggested that the integration of AI in the education system empowers instructors to customize content and activities to align with each student’s strengths, learning pace and areas of improvement. This tailored approach maximizes student understanding, engagement and performance in e-learning environments. By leveraging AI technologies, educational institutions can create adaptive learning environments that cater to the diverse needs of students, enhance the quality of education delivery and foster a more engaging and practical learning experience. AI’s role in education continues to evolve, offering innovative solutions to optimize teaching methodologies, student support and overall educational outcomes (Kovari, 2024).

Seo et al. (2021) propose that AI has the potential to revolutionize education, enhancing its accessibility, success and efficiency for students worldwide. AI not only aids students in understanding and learning but also provides real-time assistance to higher institutional students. AI-powered tutoring systems can identify specific difficulties or challenges that students encounter, enabling educators to offer targeted explanations, feedback and additional resources to assist students in overcoming these obstacles (Pataranutaporn et al., 2021). Moreover, AI can simplify the grading process for assessments like multiple-choice questions. Automating grading tasks enables educators to save time and establish a more effective feedback loop (Sallam et al., 2024). This loop allows students to monitor their progress and empowers them to make essential improvements in their educational path. Recognizing that the effective incorporation of AI technology in education necessitates deliberate design, ethical considerations and continual assessment is crucial. While AI holds significant promise, it cannot replace the role of human teachers. Instead, technology should be viewed as a supplementary tool to enhance and enrich the teaching and learning experience.

AI is not inherently frightening. When used with integrity and responsibility, it can enhance the effectiveness and performance of individuals and institutions. The relationship between academic achievement and AI in education is continuously evolving. It is set to undergo further transformation as AI technology progresses and becomes more widespread in the educational landscape. This paradigm shift presents educators and students with a spectrum of opportunities and challenges. Educators must adeptly adapt to leverage the benefits of AI, navigating complexities while ensuring that the human touch in education remains paramount (Abulibdeh et al., 2024). The fusion of AI technology and education holds the potential to revolutionize conventional teaching methodologies, offering tailored learning experiences and elevating academic performance. By embracing AI as a supportive tool and recognizing its evolving role in education, educators can harness the transformative power of technology to cultivate a more effective and engaging learning environment for students in the years ahead.

## Methods

This section discusses the procedure for collecting and analyzing data. To achieve the research objectives, our study used the inductive approach, which does not need strong support from preexisting concepts and theories (Khan and Sarkar, 2024). We have examined the role of responsible AI for academic integrity among Chinese participants using a qualitative approach to discover the in-depth results that have not been covered by quantitative research. According to Mukumbang (2023), the application of philosophies such as positivism, realism, interpretivism, and pragmatism is closely tied to the investigator’s adoption of a specific research objective. In this study, an interpretivism approach has been embraced, placing emphasis on personal meanings and motives as the central focus of the research. By adopting an interpretivism approach, the study seeks to delve into the subjective interpretations and contextual understandings of individuals, acknowledging the significance of personal perspectives and motivations in shaping their experiences. This approach aligns with the idea that reality is socially constructed and that individuals actively contribute to the creation of meaning within their environments.

To gather rich and nuanced data that captures the intricacies of personal meanings and motives, potential respondents have been proactively invited to participate in the study. This proactive engagement strategy reflects a deliberate effort to involve individuals who can offer valuable insights and diverse perspectives, enhancing the depth and breadth of the research findings. Through this approach, the study seeks to not only understand the explicit experiences and viewpoints of participants but also to uncover underlying meanings and motivations that drive their actions and perceptions. By embracing interpretivism and actively engaging with respondents, the research aims to generate comprehensive insights that illuminate the complexities of personal interpretations and motivations within the context of the study.

Using a convenience sampling technique, this study aims to collect data from a readily available pool of participants efficiently. By using this method, authors can access a diverse range of respondents with relative ease. As part of the data collection process, respondents were queried about their previous encounters with AI within academic contexts. This initial filter question serves to gauge respondents’ familiarity and experiences with AI, providing valuable insights into their perspectives and interactions with the technology in an educational setting. Verifying the information provided and ensuring consensus through cross-checking are crucial aspects of conducting a qualitative study (Fernando et al., 2012). To achieve the research objectives, a well-defined plan and process are essential, which the research technique offers. The inductive approach is used in this study, as no prior assumptions are made regarding the frequency of AI usage by respondents. The investigation analyzes information from respondents through thematic analysis (Fernando et al., 2022). In this study, the authors have utilized a mono-qualitative approach, aiming to capture descriptive responses that provide in-depth comprehension and minimize the potential for incomplete information often present in quantitative data. The type of data being collected will dictate the research strategy, providing a framework for using various data collection and analysis techniques (Carter et al., 2023). The authors have opted for a crosssectional time horizon, as responses will be gathered within a limited time frame, allowing for a snapshot of perspectives at a specific point in time.

Most of the respondents feel motivated to use AI to reduce repetitive labor and improve accuracy. They also believe it can enhance their research and teaching abilities, as well as provide more comprehensive data support and decision-making references. Some respondents highlighted the importance of features such as easy access to question banks, receiving intelligent feedback and deepening subject knowledge. Many participants found AI valuable for providing personalized learning experiences and feedback tailored to individual needs. Kumar et al. (2023) discusses how AI can assist students in tracking previously completed coursework, applying that knowledge to course planning, enabling personalized learning and facilitating review processes. Despite the motivation to use AI, some participants expressed concerns about accuracy and data security risks. While acknowledging the benefits AI offers in terms of efficiency and effectiveness. These individuals also recognized the importance of ensuring that AI systems are reliable and secure, particularly when handling sensitive data and making critical decisions based on AI-generated insights. Addressing these concerns is crucial to building trust in AI technologies and maximizing their potential in educational settings.

Evaluating the responses for areas being most used for AI, it is found that most of the participants used it for data analysis, model optimization, collecting academic resources, assistance in paper writing, e-learning platform and research for teaching material. Based on our findings, participants leveraged AI to analyze and interpret data to derive insights and make informed decisions in academic research and teaching. Furthermore, AI was employed to optimize models and algorithms, enhancing the efficiency and accuracy of various processes in academic tasks. AI tools were used to gather, organize and access academic resources such as research papers, articles and educational materials more effectively. Participants used AI for assistance in drafting and refining academic papers, proofreading, translation, optimizing writing processes and improving the quality of written content. AI technologies were integrated into e-learning platforms to enhance online teaching and learning experiences, providing personalized learning pathways and interactive educational content. In addition, AI was used in conducting research to identify relevant research and teaching materials, curate educational content and enhance the quality of teaching resources used in academic settings.

The findings align with prior research, as noted by Pataranutaporn et al. (2021), who highlighted that integrating AI into education empowers instructors to tailor activities and lessons to individual students’ learning styles, paces and weaknesses. This customization enhances e-learning experiences, optimizes knowledge acquisition and boosts student engagement. In addition, participants reported using AI for creative design, digital art, NLP and other tasks, with applications varying based on their academic focus. For instance, college students used AI to assist them with paper writing and accessing academic resources. In contrast, lecturers used it for model optimization, tutoring material support, student management, teaching evaluation, course planning, code writing, data analysis, machine learning, network security, research and completing e-learning tasks.

Respondents also noted the potential for more effective implementation of AI functions in the future, acknowledging current limitations in power and effectiveness. ## Discussion

This section discusses the responses to the research question regarding the application of this knowledge in the field of education. Participants indicated that AI could be effectively used in education across various domains, including online teaching platforms, virtual laboratories, intelligent scoring systems, providing competent assistance to students and conducting comprehensive performance evaluations. The versatility of AI in educational settings is evident through its potential applications, such as automated evaluation, facial recognition systems, intelligent online teaching modules, teaching assistance, generating art, image style conversion, creative assistance, implementing intelligent academic management systems and creating virtual learning environments. Moreover, AI can play a pivotal role in enhancing educational activities by facilitating student profiling, intelligent tutoring, automated evaluation processes, intelligent algorithm design, construction of machine learning models and development of intelligent systems. By leveraging AI technologies in education, institutions can enhance teaching methodologies, personalize learning experiences, streamline administrative tasks and create dynamic learning environments that cater to the diverse needs of students. The integration of AI in education holds the promise of revolutionizing traditional educational paradigms and fostering innovative approaches to teaching and learning.

For instance, Seo et al. (2021) suggest that AI holds the promise to transform education, enhancing its accessibility, effectiveness and success for students globally. AI not only aids students in understanding and learning but also provides real-time assistance. Participants generally agreed that when AI is implemented within specified regulations, these applications align with academic integrity standards. Some respondents specifically noted that technologies like voice recognition and intelligent monitoring could serve as valuable teaching aids. However, a few participants expressed concerns about the reliability of implementing such technologies within the domestic education sector. The potential of AI to revolutionize education lies in its ability to personalize learning experiences, provide immediate feedback and offer tailored support to students. By ensuring that AI applications are ethically developed and effectively regulated, educational institutions can harness the power of AI to enhance teaching and learning practices, ultimately benefiting students on a global scale.

When assessing the responsibility of educational institutions in supporting students to uphold academic integrity while leveraging AI technology conscientiously, respondents emphasized the crucial role of institutes in guiding students on ethical behavior. This guidance is seen as essential for students to make informed decisions and adhere to the principles of academic integrity. Participants suggested that educational institutions should actively educate students on what constitutes ethical behavior and what does not, fostering a culture of integrity within academic settings. By providing clear guidelines and ethical frameworks, institutions can empower students to uphold academic honesty. Moreover, respondents highlighted the importance of institutions in bolstering academic integrity education, enhancing AI detection technologies, preventing instances of academic misconduct and fostering the positive development of students. Strengthening these aspects can contribute to creating a conducive environment where academic integrity is valued and upheld.

Respondents emphasized the importance of implementing improved performance evaluation methods, such as project reports and practical projects, to inspire students to assess and enhance their creativity and practical skills. Encouraging students to engage in reflective practices through these evaluation techniques can motivate them to strive for excellence and personal growth. In addition, participants highlighted the responsibility of educational institutions in guiding college students on how to avoid academic misconduct. This guidance is crucial for fostering a culture of academic integrity within academic settings. This perspective is supported by Surahman and Wang (2022), who suggest that adherence to academic integrity is influenced not only by personal factors but also by institutional conditions. Factors such as the pressure to achieve high scores or a competitive academic environment can contribute to instances of academic dishonesty among students. Therefore, institutions must educate stakeholders on the ethical use of AI in educational settings.

In alignment with the discussions outlined above, instructors play a crucial role in enhancing teaching methodologies, refining their instructional skills and promoting comprehensive student development. By establishing and standardizing effective teaching practices, educators can create an environment conducive to learning and personal growth. This approach not only enhances the educational experience for students but also contributes to their holistic development. Respondents also emphasized the importance of educationa institutions in ensuring the ethical and safe implementation of AI technologies in academic settings. This involves enhancing supervision, developing relevant policies and fostering the responsible and healthy integration of AI in education. By addressing ethical concerns and promoting safety measures, institutions can create a secure and supportive environment for leveraging AI technologies in teaching and learning. The parallels between the literature findings and the responses from participants highlight a convergence of perspectives regarding the motivation, benefits and challenges associated with integrating AI in education. Both sources underscore the potential benefits of AI in enhancing educational outcomes, the importance of promoting ethical practices and the need to address challenges related to academic integrity, student development and ethical considerations. This alignment reinforces the significance of fostering a balanced and responsible approach to leveraging AI in educational settings for the betterment of students and the academic community as a whole.

## Limitations and future research

Artificial technology is continuously developing, and this will not remain the same in the future as people use it now. In the future, there is the possibility that AI-related ethical challenges will be solved, and no integrity issue will exist while responsible artificial technology is integrated into academics. However, there is also the chance that it may severely impact the honesty and integrity of the academics with time. Our study has focused on qualitative data to understand AI users’ perceptions and has not done much to gain access to quantitative data. Due to limited access to AI literacy and expertise, we have collected data using convenience sampling and not equal probability. Our study has limitations on data analysis using typical statistical analysis. Our findings also deal with uncertainty and acknowledge it as the limitation of this study. AI has different functions, and each academic field might use it for different platforms and purposes. In this study, we did not specifically specify the type of AI and its purpose. Besides that, there is no clear rule on how to use AI in academics. In the future, the University of the Ministry of Education shall set the AI standard to complete the research and assignment. It is also to consider the policy of human–machine interaction by replacing university professors teaching in the classroom. We have discussed AI in general terms. Our study has several limitations that can guide potential future research directions:

Uncertainty of future AI developments for academics. Our findings acknowledge that AI technology is rapidly evolving, leading to uncertainties about how ethical challenges related to AI integration in academics will be resolved in the future.

� Our study does not explore deeper investigation on AI features types and purposes.

Our study did not specify the types of AI technologies being used or their specific purposes within different academic fields. This lack of specificity limits the depth of understanding regarding the diverse applications of AI in education.

Artificial technology is continuously evolving, and its future applications may differ significantly from current usage patterns. In the years to come, it is plausible that AI-related ethical challenges will be effectively addressed, potentially eliminating integrity issues in the integration of artificial technology in academic settings. However, there remains a possibility that AI advancements could have adverse effects on academic honesty and integrity over time. This uncertainty represents a vital limitation of the current research. AI serves a multitude of functions, and its utilization can vary across different academic disciplines. Unfortunately, our study did not delve into the specifics of AI types and their intended purposes within distinct academic domains. This lack of specificity limits the depth of our understanding regarding the diverse applications of AI in education.

Moreover, a clear set of guidelines on the appropriate use of AI in academic contexts was not established within the scope of our study. As AI technologies continue to shape the educational landscape, it becomes imperative for regulatory bodies such as the university or the Ministry of Education to establish standardized AI protocols to facilitate research and assignments effectively. In addition, considerations regarding the policy implications of human–machine interaction, including the potential replacement of university professors in the classroom, were noted but not extensively explored in our study.

Moving forward, these limitations can inform potential future research directions. Uncertainty of future AI developments. Future studies should address the evolving nature of AI technologies and the implications for academic integrity, considering how ethical challenges may be resolved as AI continues to advance. Exploration of AI features, types and purposes. Our study does not explore the AI features and types. Future studies should delve deeper into the various types of AI technologies used in different academic fields to gain a comprehensive understanding of their applications and impact. By addressing these limitations and focusing on these potential future research directions, scholars can contribute to a more nuanced understanding of AI’s role in academia and develop strategies to ensure its ethical and effective integration in educational settings.

## Suggestion for future research

In this study, we conclude that AI will undoubtedly be widely used in the future. With the continuous development and advancement of AI, university stakeholders will have numerous new aspects to identify, evaluate and explore in academic settings. The fundamental AI capabilities include recognizing and matching patterns, decision-making, conceptual linkage and reasoning, implementation and sequential control, planning and problem-solving. These capabilities form the basis of an AI-based smart learning system that encompasses intelligent tutoring and virtual reality. Future research may focus on integrating AI to enhance social and emotional learning in students by recognizing emotions, offering empathetic responses and providing emotional well-being counseling. We suggest that future studies should concentrate on developing clear guidelines or policies for the ethical integration of AI in academia to ensure responsible and effective use of AI technologies in educational environments. In addition, future research should investigate the policy implications of AI in education, particularly regarding human–machine interaction and the potential role of AI in teaching. Exploring these areas can illuminate the impacts of AI on traditional educational practices and the evolving role of educators in the future.

Furthermore, we recommend that future studies explore the development of ethical frameworks for AI usage in academia to tackle challenges related to academic integrity and guarantee that AI technologies are implemented responsibly and transparently. We argue that future research direction can focus on the development of NLP models. It holds immense potential to revolutionize how literature reviews, data searches and academic article summaries are conducted. By leveraging NLP technologies, scholars and students can streamline the process of gathering information, extracting key insights and synthesizing complex academic resources efficiently. The integration of a Lecture-Bot prototype, powered by AI and NLP technologies, can enhance the students’ learning experience. The Lecture-Bot can provide interactive lectures, personalized learning paths and real-time feedback to students, promoting engagement and knowledge retention. In conclusion, the fusion of NLP models, AI technologies and innovative educational tools like the Lecture-Bot prototype has the potential to transform traditional learning paradigms, optimize research processes and enhance student achievement through personalized and engaging learning experiences.
